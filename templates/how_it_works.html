How days are assigned

Every student is placed as high on their own list as the available seats allow, and ties are broken by a fixed random draw.

Summary for students

Rank the days in your true order of preference, with your first choice at the top.

When sign-ups close, each student receives the highest-ranked day that still has room. When more students want a day than it has seats, a random draw decides.

Submitting early confers no advantage, and ranking a day above where you truly want it cannot improve your result.

Days you cannot attend

Mark a day “I really can't do this day” only for a genuine conflict. You will never be assigned a day you have marked. If every day you can attend is full, your instructor assigns you a day personally, with your note in front of them. No other student is moved to make room, so marking a day you could attend offers no advantage. Notes are visible only to your instructor.

Fairness

  • Not first-come, first-served. The time a ranking was submitted is never used.
  • A fixed random draw breaks ties. Each sheet has its own draw, and each student's place in it is fixed: it does not change when classmates rank, revise their choices, or mark days. The same rankings always produce the same schedule, so the draw cannot be repeated until a preferred result appears. The instructor chooses the tie-breaking method before making the schedule and may adjust assignments by hand afterwards; every change is recorded in the sheet's history.
  • Honest ranking is the best strategy. No other order can earn you a better day, and marking days you cannot attend never affects other students.

Frequently asked questions

Why didn't I receive my first choice?
Seats are limited. When more students choose a day than it has seats, the random draw determines who receives it, and everyone else receives their next choice that still has room.

Could ranking strategically help me?
No. Ranking in your true order of preference is always at least as good as any other order, regardless of how others rank.

When will I know my day?
When your instructor publishes the schedule, your day appears on your class's sign-up page.

Technical details: the assignment algorithm

Schedules are produced with deferred acceptance (the Gale–Shapley algorithm, extended to days with several seats), the same method used by many school districts to assign students to schools with limited places. It proceeds in rounds:

  1. Each student applies to the highest day on their list that has not yet declined them.
  2. Each day holds the applicants it has room for, in the order set by its random draw, and declines the rest. No hold is final until the process ends.
  3. Declined students apply to their next choice, and the process repeats until no student is declined.

The result is stable: no student prefers a day that would have admitted them ahead of someone placed there. It is also strategy-proof for students, which is why honest ranking is always the best approach.

Example. Four students and two days, with two seats each:

  • Ana, Ben, and Cleo rank Day 1 first, then Day 2.
  • Dev ranks Day 2 first, then Day 1.
  • Day 1's random draw orders the students Ben, Ana, Cleo, Dev.

In the first round, Ana, Ben, and Cleo apply to Day 1, which has two seats; following its draw, it holds Ben and Ana and declines Cleo. Dev applies to Day 2 and is held. In the second round, Cleo applies to Day 2, which still has room. Final schedule: Day 1, Ana and Ben; Day 2, Cleo and Dev. Cleo did not receive her first choice, but no change could give it to her without displacing a student the draw placed ahead of her.
