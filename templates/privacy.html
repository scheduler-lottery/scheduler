Privacy

What this site keeps, who can see it, and how it goes away.

In short

  • Students: your instructor sees your ranking and notes. Classmates see names and days only if your instructor turns that on. Nobody else sees anything.
  • Instructors: only you can open your sheets on this site — other instructors can't, and the site gives the person who runs it no way to either (details below).
  • Only names and email addresses are kept from a class list; everything else in the file is thrown away.

What's stored

  • Instructors: your email address, your time zone (for dates in downloads), and the sign-up sheets you create.
  • Class lists: each student's name and email address — nothing else. When an instructor uploads a Canvas export, every other column (grades, activity, IDs) is discarded on the spot and never saved.
  • Rankings: each student's order of days, any day they said they can't do, and any note they wrote for their instructor.
  • The schedule, once the instructor makes it.
  • Earlier versions of each sheet, kept so an instructor can undo mistakes.

Who can see it

  • Each instructor sees only their own sheets. No other instructor can open them, and the site has no screen that shows one instructor's sheets to anyone else — including the person who runs the site.
  • Students see their own ranking, and their own day once the schedule is published. If the instructor turns it on, signed-in students in the same class can also see everyone's names and days — but never anyone's notes, can't-do days, or who hasn't ranked.
  • The person who runs this site sees only site-wide totals (how many sheets and students, how many emails were sent today). Like anyone operating a database, they could technically read it directly in the hosting provider's console; they don't, and the site itself offers no way to.

Signing in

There are no passwords. You get a one-time code by email; students without an email on the class list choose a PIN instead, which is stored scrambled. A single cookie keeps you signed in on that browser for up to 90 days (or 20 minutes of inactivity, if you tick “shared computer”). There's no tracking, no advertising, and no analytics. Fonts load from Google Fonts.

So students can find their own name, after someone with the sign-up's link types three letters, up to five matching names from that class list appear. Email addresses are never shown.

Email

When those can't reach a student, their instructor can send them a sign-in link from the instructor's own email instead: the site writes the message, and it goes out through the instructor's own email account, not through this site. Instructors can also get a backup of a sheet by email (for example, when they close sign-ups); a backup holds the class list, rankings and notes, and goes only to that instructor's own address.

How long it's kept

  • A sheet stays until its instructor deletes it. A deleted sheet can be brought back for 30 days, then it's gone for good.
  • Sign-in codes expire after 30 minutes, and the one-click link in a student's code email works once, within a day. Records used to limit how many emails are sent hold scrambled addresses, not real ones, and are erased after two days.
  • Earlier versions of a sheet are capped per sheet; older ones are removed automatically.

Where it lives

The site runs on Vercel, the data is stored in a Supabase (Postgres) database, and email is sent through Gmail. Everything travels encrypted (HTTPS).

Your school's rules

The site keeps only what it needs: names, email addresses, rankings and notes. If your school has rules about student information in outside tools (in the US, for example, FERPA), check with your school before using it.
